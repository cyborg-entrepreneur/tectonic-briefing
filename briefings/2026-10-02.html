Who carries the decision

Governor Gavin Newsom signed a law on 30 September that bars California employers from relying solely on an automated system to discipline or fire workers; a person must corroborate the decision. The same day he vetoed a bill that would have protected nurses who override AI recommendations they judge unsafe. On 1 October Israel’s Supreme Court told Balad leader Sami Abou Shehadeh that a majority of its justices would uphold his disqualification from the 27 October election; he withdrew overnight, and no ruling was issued. TV Globo cancelled Brazil’s last presidential debate before Sunday’s first round after both front-runners declined to attend.

Washington asked European governments to release 120 million barrels of emergency diesel over six months and warned France and Germany of a possible US export ban. Euro-area inflation rose to 3.8 percent in September, with energy prices up 18.8 percent. Vladimir Putin put the cost of Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries at about 1 percent of GDP, and an arbitral tribunal upheld Ghana’s $393.09 million tax claim against Tullow.

Ethiopia expelled ten Eritrean diplomats and closed its embassy in Asmara after overnight blasts in Addis Ababa, and Eritrea severed ties. Guinea-Bissau’s junta barred the return of the president it ousted last November. A Russian drone hit a Kyiv school a day after another struck the institute that houses the city’s research reactor. A US official said a third carrier group was heading toward Iran, and Seoul threatened measures against Kyiv over a disclosed prisoner transfer.

Nature published a Google DeepMind method for watermarking AI-designed proteins, an AI that beat the most decorated human Stratego player, film of Asgard archaea crawling, a new genetic switch for fetal haemoglobin and evidence that sugar worsens antibiotic damage to the gut. IDC counted almost 25,000 humanoid robots shipped in six months, about 69 percent of them to labs, classrooms, exhibitions and government data centres.

The Himlung avalanche toll reached 15 Nepali workers. Hong Kong’s heat warnings remain advisory after a record 36.9°C, and the Atlantic had its first hurricane-free September since 1994. Chile mined its least copper in a month since 2011, Colombia banned imports made with forced labour, and a Mongabay investigation traced Myanmar’s conflict-zone minerals to China through Thai firms. Nottingham staff began a strike that runs into December, and protests in Pristina against the Hague convictions of former Kosovo Liberation Army leaders reached a sixteenth day.

Geopolitics & Security

30 SEP–2 OCT 2026 · ETHIOPIA / ERITREA / EGYPT

Ethiopia expels Eritrean diplomats and closes its Asmara embassy, and Eritrea severs ties

OBS: Ethiopia declared ten Eritrean diplomats persona non grata on 1 October, including the chargé d’affaires, and closed its embassy in Asmara, citing Eritrean “direct and indirect acts of hostility and belligerence,” Al Jazeera reported. Eritrea said it had “no option but to reciprocate in kind and sever all diplomatic ties.” Ethiopia also expelled an Egyptian diplomat, and Egypt expelled the Ethiopian embassy’s counsellor. The moves followed overnight blasts in Addis Ababa that the armed opposition alliance called drone strikes on military headquarters. Afar’s deputy governor said Eritrean soldiers had crossed into the region, which Eritrea denies.

INF: The 29 September issue reported Ethiopia’s army chief naming Eritrea, Sudan’s army and Egypt as backers of the rebellion; two of the three are now in formal diplomatic disputes with Addis Ababa. Severing ties also removes the channel through which either government could clarify a border incident.

HYP: Over the next six weeks, an independently confirmed clash between Ethiopian and Eritrean units would mark an interstate war. Restored consular contact, or an African Union envoy received in both capitals, would show the rupture held below that line.

30 SEP–1 OCT 2026 · UKRAINE / RUSSIA

Russian drones hit a Kyiv school, a Dnipro bridge and the institute that houses Kyiv’s research reactor

OBS: A Russian drone struck a Kyiv school on 1 October while children and staff sheltered; its warhead did not explode and no one was hurt, AP reported. Two drones hit a bridge across the Dnipro, halting traffic. On the evening of 30 September a drone hit the Institute for Nuclear Research, whose VVR-M research reactor has been shut down with its core unloaded since February 2022; a fire was put out and radiation stayed at background levels, Ukraine’s nuclear regulator said, Ukrinform reported. Ukraine’s air force counted 3,177 Russian drones in September through the 29th, against 2,850 in August, Al Jazeera reported.

INF: The September count is about 11 percent above August’s. A reactor with an unloaded core carries little radiological risk, but the strike brings a nuclear facility into the pattern of attacks on the capital.

HYP: Over the next month, further strikes on Kyiv’s bridges or on nuclear and research sites would show the target set widening. An October drone count below September’s would point to a peak.

DEEP DIVE · READ: O

From accusation to rupture

On 27 September Ethiopia’s army chief, Field Marshal Birhanu Jula, named Eritrea, Sudan’s armed forces and Egypt as backers of the forces fighting the federal government. Within five days Ethiopia had expelled Eritrean and Egyptian diplomats and closed its embassy in Asmara, and Eritrea had severed ties. Sudan’s army calls the accusation against it baseless.

The break undoes the 2018 rapprochement between Abiy Ahmed and Isaias Afwerki that ended two decades of hostility after the 1998–2000 border war. Federal forces were reported about 40 kilometres from Mekelle, with Tigrayan leaders leaving the city, according to Reuters reporting carried by Al Jazeera. Electricity and internet are cut across Tigray, so neither the advance nor the claims about Eritrean troops in Afar can be checked; more than 80,000 Afar residents are trapped in conflict zones.

Each government now names the other’s hostility as a cause of the fighting inside Ethiopia, and the channel that could test those claims is closed. That is Category Collapse in a specific form: the distinction between civil war and interstate conflict still governs how outsiders respond, while the parties act as though it no longer holds. None of the claims has been independently verified.

Over the next six weeks, three observations would discriminate. An independently confirmed engagement between Ethiopian and Eritrean units would mark an interstate war. Federal forces entering Mekelle with no cross-border incident would show Addis Ababa settling the internal war first. An African Union or IGAD envoy received in both capitals would show the rupture kept short of fighting.

03 · Technological

Technology

29 SEP–1 OCT 2026 · CHINA / GLOBAL · ROBOTICS

IDC counts almost 25,000 humanoid robots shipped in six months, most of them to labs and showrooms

OBS: Humanoid robot makers shipped nearly 25,000 units in the first half of 2026, up 432.1 percent, according to IDC data reported by TechNode on 29 September; Chinese customers bought 77.9 percent, and Chinese vendors supplied more than 95 percent, Digitimes reported. AgiBot shipped more than 8,600 units and Unitree about 5,000, John Koetsier reported from the IDC figures, which put the average price just under $30,000, down from about $37,000. About 69 percent of units went to research labs, classrooms, exhibitions and government data centres, down from 83.8 percent.

INF: The 29 September issue reported five million industrial robots at work. Humanoids remain a sliver of that base, and most still serve research and demonstration; the falling price and the shrinking research share will show whether they move into paid work.

HYP: A research share below half and named industrial customers placing repeat orders would show that move; rising counts with a steady research share would not.

TechNode · 29 September · Digitimes · 1 October · John Koetsier · 1 October

30 SEP 2026 · NATURE · ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

An AI built for hidden information defeats the most decorated human Stratego player

OBS: A paper in Nature on 30 September by Samuel Sokota and colleagues at Carnegie Mellon, NYU, Stanford and MIT introduces Ataraxos, an AI for Stratego, a board wargame in which each player hides the identity of their pieces. Ataraxos “defeated the most decorated human Stratego player of all time by a large margin,” the first superhuman result in the game’s history, the authors write, “while consuming orders of magnitude less compute and data than previous efforts.” The same methods produced a superhuman AI for Barrage Stratego and leading play in Hanabi and dou dizhu.

INF: Large amounts of hidden information defeated the search methods behind earlier game-playing AI, including multimillion-dollar industrial efforts on Stratego. A low-cost method that works in adversarial, cooperative and team games turns such play into an engineering problem. In each game the rules and payoffs are fixed and known to every player.

HYP: Published results in settings where the rules or payoffs change during play would test how far the approach reaches beyond games.

Nature · Sokota et al., 30 September

30 SEP–2 OCT 2026 · NATURE · BIOSECURITY

Google DeepMind watermarks AI-designed proteins, and the watermarked binders still bind

OBS: A paper in Nature on 30 September by David Stutz and colleagues at Google DeepMind introduces SynthIDBio, which embeds a statistical watermark in the sequences of AI-designed proteins and, through a fine-tuned AlphaFold 3 model, in predicted structures. Watermarked binders against VEGF-A, the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain and PD-L1 performed like unwatermarked designs, with “near-perfect watermark detection accuracy.” Google said robustness “against deliberate tampering” remains a key challenge, MIXED reported on 2 October.

INF: The mark records origin in the molecule rather than in a file’s metadata, which matters once a design leaves the lab as DNA or protein. A mark that can be stripped helps attribute careless use; against deliberate misuse it helps only if synthesis providers check for it.

HYP: A DNA synthesis provider or protein database adopting detection would show the watermark becoming infrastructure. Published removal methods without a countermeasure would confine it to voluntary labelling.

Nature · Stutz et al., 30 September · MIXED · 2 October

OBS: The Trump administration told France and Germany they could face “a potential US diesel export ban” if they did not release emergency inventories, Reuters reported on 1 October; a European official said the United States had asked for “120 million barrels of diesel over the next six months.” Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Europe should “make additional supplies immediately available.” France proposed instead that International Energy Agency members release 50 million barrels of diesel and 50 million of crude, Al Jazeera reported on 2 October. DEEP DIVE · READ: O

Who spends the diesel

Three of the largest suppliers of diesel made decisions this week that point in different directions. Washington asked European allies to release 120 million barrels over six months and threatened to restrict US exports if France and Germany refused. At the Valdai forum on 1 October Vladimir Putin said Russia had enough diesel for its own needs but that sanctions would keep any surplus off the world market, and estimated the cost of Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries at about 1 percent of GDP. The 1 October issue reported that Chinese refiners had suspended October fuel exports to rebuild domestic stocks.

Each holder is guarding its own exposure. Russia’s diesel is constrained by sanctions on one side and by drones on the other, and Putin rejected an exchange in which Russia would stop striking Ukrainian ships and Ukraine would stop striking refineries: “How is our diesel supposed to reach the global market with sanctions still in place? It can’t.” The United States, which exports about 1.5 million barrels a day, is using its position as a supplier to press for releases from stocks it does not control.

Europe is exposed on both sides. Euro-area energy prices rose 18.8 percent in the year to September, and European diesel averaged a record 2.24 euros a litre, Al Jazeera reported. Releasing emergency stocks would lower prices now and leave less in reserve for a winter in which the Strait of Hormuz remains contested. The stocks exist for a physical shortage. Buffer Collapse names the risk: a reserve drawn down to ease a price may be missing when a cargo fails to arrive.

Over the next six weeks, three observations would discriminate. An IEA or G7 release that names diesel volumes and a schedule, as France proposed, would show allies settling on a shared draw. A formal US order restricting diesel exports would show Washington enforcing the request at a cost to its refiners’ customers. Neither, with European prices easing, would show the market settling the dispute and the stocks kept for winter.

Meduza · 1 October · Al Jazeera · 2 October · 1 October issue · Chinese refiners suspend exports

05 · Scientific

Science

30 SEP 2026 · NATURE · EVOLUTION

Asgard archaea crawl on actin-filled protrusions, as amoebae and white blood cells do

OBS: A study in Nature on 30 September, led from the University of Vienna by Philipp Radler with Christa Schleper and Michael Sixt as senior authors, filmed living cells of two Asgard archaea. The cells changed shape within minutes and extended and retracted protrusions at 1.5 to 4.8 micrometres a minute; after adhering to glass, they used the protrusions to crawl, and actin inhibitors stopped the movement. The authors call this plasticity and motility “unique features among prokaryotes” that “might have been crucial for the emergence of the first eukaryotic cells.”

INF: Eukaryotic cells are thought to have arisen from a partnership between an Asgard archaeon and the bacterium that became the mitochondrion. A partner able to change shape and reach toward other cells fits that merger better than a rigid one, and the study shows the capacity in living cells rather than inferring it from genes.

HYP: Imaging of Asgard cells in contact with bacteria, and genetic tools that remove their actin, would test whether the protrusions mediate close partnerships.

Nature · Radler et al., 30 September

30 SEP 2026 · NATURE · GENETICS

A genetic study of 28,279 people finds a new switch for fetal haemoglobin

OBS: A multi-ancestry genetic study of fetal haemoglobin levels in 28,279 people, published in Nature on 30 September by Chun-Jie Guo and colleagues with Vijay Sankaran of Boston Children’s Hospital as senior author, identified 91 independent associations across 12 genomic regions. In one new region, the variant rs1010474-C lowers expression of the gene BACH2 and raises fetal haemoglobin; inhibiting BACH2 had the same effect. BACH2 restrains the γ-globin genes and its loss lets the protein NRF2 bind them; editing the two proteins’ shared binding sites switched γ-globin on or off independently of BCL11A, whose enhancer is edited in the first approved CRISPR therapy for sickle cell disease. Co-authors include researchers at Muhimbili University of Health and Allied Sciences in Dar es Salaam and at blood centres in Brazil.

INF: Raising fetal haemoglobin relieves sickle cell disease and β-thalassaemia, which fall most heavily on sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. The approved gene-editing therapy is costly and acts through BCL11A alone; a second, independent switch that a drug might inhibit gives developers another route.

HYP: A BACH2-targeting compound entering preclinical testing, or edits at the BACH2–NRF2 sites in patients’ blood stem cells, would show the finding moving toward treatment.

Nature · Guo et al., 30 September

30 SEP 2026 · NATURE · MICROBIOME

Sugar eaten during antibiotic treatment predicts worse gut disruption in transplant patients

OBS: Researchers at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and NYU Langone, led by Anqi Dai with Jonas Schluter and Jonathan Peled as senior authors, tracked 9,419 meals eaten by 173 patients hospitalised for blood stem cell transplantation, Nature published on 30 September. Eating sweets and sugars while on antibiotics predicted lower microbial diversity and a greater expansion of Enterococcus, a gut bacterium that can cause disease, and added sucrose prolonged that expansion in mice. The authors write that avoiding simple sugars during antibiotic treatment “may mitigate microbiota disruption” and call for independent cohorts.

INF: A hospital controls its menus far more easily than it controls a patient’s microbiome. The study links a specific food group to a specific harm in a specific treatment window, which is the evidence a dietary change on a transplant ward would need.

HYP: A randomised trial of low-sugar diets during antibiotic courses, and replication outside transplant units, would test the effect and its reach.

Nature · Dai et al., 30 September

26 SEP–2 OCT 2026 · NEPAL

The Himlung avalanche toll reaches 15 Nepali workers who were preparing the mountain for foreign clients

OBS: Rescuers recovered eight more bodies on 1 October from the base camp of Himlung Himal, a 7,126-metre peak in Manang, bringing the toll to 15, the Kathmandu Post reported; one worker remains missing. HYP: Compensation to the families, new rules on early-season route work, and the ILO’s recovery figures for the flood districts will show whether these risks are being priced into the industry or left with the workers.

07 · Environmental

Environment & Ecology

30 SEP 2026 · NATURE · COASTS

Waves decide where mangroves can establish on open coasts

OBS: A study in Nature on 30 September by Sarah Wells and colleagues at UNSW Sydney, Tulane University and the University of Oldenburg modelled 20 years of hindcast wave data at 400 coastal sites worldwide. By identifying “windows of opportunity” in which waves are low enough for seedlings to establish, the model predicted the presence or absence of mangroves with 78.2 percent accuracy, and modest changes in wave forcing changed whether sites were suitable.

INF: Mangrove restoration often fails where seedlings are planted on coasts they cannot hold. A mechanism tied to wave windows gives planners a test to apply before planting, and it makes future wave climate, which shifts with storm tracks and sea level, part of the question of where mangroves can persist.

HYP: Restoration programmes that select sites by wave windows and report seedling survival against programmes that do not would test the model in the field.

Nature · Wells et al., 30 September

30 SEP–2 OCT 2026 · ATLANTIC / EASTERN PACIFIC

The Atlantic passes its first September without a hurricane since 1994 as the eastern Pacific runs hot

OBS: No Atlantic storm reached hurricane strength in September, the first such September since 1994; 1972 and 1962 are the only other such Septembers since 1950, CNN reported on 2 October. Tropical Storm Fay came within 4 mph of hurricane strength. The eastern Pacific has produced 20 named storms, three of them Category 5 hurricanes, including Polo. Record wind shear linked to El Niño has suppressed Atlantic development since summer; only 1907 and 1914 ended an Atlantic season without a hurricane, Andrew Wulfeck of the Florida Public Radio Emergency Network noted on 1 October.

INF: The 29 September issue reported NOAA’s estimate of a better than 90 percent chance of a very strong El Niño. The same event is moving cyclone risk from the Caribbean and the US Gulf coast to Mexico’s Pacific coast, so insurers and emergency agencies that plan around the Atlantic season face a quiet year there and a severe one on Mexico’s Pacific coast.

HYP: An October Atlantic hurricane would end the streak; a season that closes on 30 November without one would match the two pre-satellite seasons.

CNN via WSVN · 2 October · WGCU / FPREN · 1 October

9 AUG–2 OCT 2026 · HONG KONG

Hong Kong’s heat warnings stay advisory after a record 36.9°C

OBS: The Hong Kong Observatory recorded 36.9°C on 9 August, the highest since records began in 1884, Mongabay reported on 2 October. The heat stress warning system, revised in August with lower thresholds and automatic triggers, issues amber, red and black alerts but does not require work to stop. Official figures record 76 heatstroke or heat-stress cases and one death in 2022–2024; a union survey found nearly 40 percent of outdoor workers worked over four hours without extra breaks during “very hot weather” warnings. “It’s just an appeal, encouragement; there’s no law,” said the head of the Association for the Rights of Industrial Accident Victims.

INF: The revised system triggers earlier but signals rather than obliges. The decision to stop stays with employers and with workers paid by the task, whom one advocate described as “scared to rest because they might be accused of being lazy.”

HYP: Legislation that makes heat warnings binding, or adds heatstroke to the list of compensable occupational diseases, would move the risk to employers. Another summer under advisory warnings would leave it with workers.

Mongabay · 2 October

08 · Institutional

Institutions & Governance

30 SEP–1 OCT 2026 · CALIFORNIA

California requires a person to confirm AI firings and declines to protect nurses who overrule AI

OBS: Governor Gavin Newsom on 30 September signed SB 947, the No Robo Bosses Act, which bars employers from relying solely on an automated decision system to discipline or fire workers, requires a human to corroborate such decisions and requires that workers be told; it takes effect on 1 July 2027, KQED reported. He also signed AB 1979, which limits AI in health care to an advisory role, and laws on AI-driven layoffs and workplace surveillance. He vetoed AB 2575, which would have barred retaliation against nurses and other health workers who override AI recommendations they judge unsafe and kept liability for harm from being shifted onto them. SB 947 revives a measure Newsom vetoed last year. “Nobody wants to be fired by a machine,” said its author, Senator Jerry McNerney.

INF: The signed laws place a person in every AI-informed firing and every clinical decision that uses AI. The vetoed bill would have protected the person who disagrees with the system.

HYP: Employer policies and union contracts written before SB 947 takes effect will show whether override protection arrives by agreement in the absence of a statute.

KQED · 1 October · CNBC · 30 September

29 SEP–4 OCT 2026 · BRAZIL / PERU

Brazil’s last debate before Sunday’s vote is cancelled after both front-runners withdraw

1–2 OCT 2026 · ISRAEL

Israel’s Supreme Court ends an Arab party leader’s candidacy without issuing a ruling

OBS: At a hearing on 1 October, a nine-justice panel of Israel’s Supreme Court indicated that a majority would uphold the Central Elections Committee’s disqualification of Balad leader Sami Abou Shehadeh from the 27 October election and urged him to withdraw rather than face a ruling; he withdrew overnight, the Times of Israel reported. The ban, under Section 7A of the election law, rested on an article he published the day after the 7 October 2023 attack; at the hearing he expressed regret and denied supporting armed struggle. Balad will still run. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, who sought the ban, criticised the court for not ruling, and the rights group Adalah said a Palestinian leader had been “forced out,” AFP reported via Dawn.

DEEP DIVE · READ: O

Required to decide, unprotected for disagreeing

California’s new laws place a person at two decision points. Under SB 947, an employer that uses an automated system to discipline or fire a worker must have a human corroborate the decision and tell the worker. Under AB 1979, AI used in health care may advise but may not replace a licensed professional’s judgment. AB 2575, which Newsom vetoed on the same day, addressed the moment when the person and the system disagree: it would have barred retaliation against nurses who override an AI recommendation they judge unsafe and stopped employers and developers from shifting liability for harm onto those workers.

Without that protection, the reviewer’s two choices carry unequal risk. Corroborating the system follows the tool the employer chose. Overriding it is a departure the worker must defend alone, and if the override goes wrong, the liability the vetoed bill would have blocked can land on the worker. The law records the decision as the person’s while the employment relation makes agreement the safer course. The veto leaves that exposure in place.

The arrangement fits Channel Decomposition: the statute keeps the form of human judgment, and the protection that would make that judgment independent sits in a bill that was not signed. Nurses who backed AB 1979 framed it as a defence of clinical judgment: “We stood up and said that A.I. must support, not replace, the clinical judgement of the nurses and other health care professionals who care for our patients,” KQED reported.

SB 947 becomes operative on 1 July 2027, which leaves a window to observe. Knowledge problems (Townsend, Hunt, McMullen & Sarasvathy, Academy of Management Annals, 2018): Ataraxos reached superhuman Stratego play under large amounts of hidden information. In the framework’s terms, hidden information in a game with fixed, shared rules is a problem of missing information about a knowable state: the opponent knows what is hidden, and the set of possible states is defined in advance. Knightian uncertainty concerns settings in which that set is itself open. The result sets a boundary on claims about AI and uncertainty: techniques that cheaply resolve hidden information do not by that fact address uncertainty. The discriminating test is performance when rules or payoffs change during play, which the paper does not report.
