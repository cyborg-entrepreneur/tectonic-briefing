3–5 OCT 2026 · ETHIOPIA / TIGRAY

Ethiopian forces take Mekelle as the TPLF says it has moved out

OBS: Government forces and allied fighters entered Mekelle, the Tigrayan capital, on Saturday 3 October, and the Tigray People’s Liberation Front said the same day that it had “temporarily moved out of Mekelle” and that the regional government would “operate from somewhere else,” Al Jazeera reported. On 4 October the leaders of Egypt, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan’s army met at New Alamein in Egypt and issued a declaration that control of the Red Sea should remain with the coastal states. The reports give no casualty or displacement figures.

INF: Friday’s issue reported federal forces about 40 kilometres from the city. Mekelle changed hands without a reported battle for it, so the TPLF’s forces remain in the field. No clash between Ethiopian and Eritrean units has been confirmed. Eritrea’s president joined a four-leader declaration that included a Red Sea clause the day after the city changed hands.

HYP: Over the next six weeks, TPLF attacks from outside the city, an interim administration seated in Mekelle, or a confirmed engagement with Eritrean units would each point to a different course for the war.

30 SEP–1 OCT 2026 · UNITED STATES · GEOTHERMAL

Fervo’s first enhanced-geothermal unit in Utah starts selling power under contract

OBS: Fervo Energy declared commercial operation on 30 September of the first 33-megawatt unit at Cape Station in Beaver County, Utah, six days after the plant synchronised to the grid and one day before its contractual date, the company said on 1 October. The plant was built and commissioned in 23 months, and Fervo “is now generating revenue under a power purchase agreement,” ThinkGeoEnergy reported; neither account names the buyer. Two more 33-megawatt units are due to reach their contractual dates by 1 January 2027, and a further 400 megawatts is under construction for 2028.

INF: Enhanced geothermal fractures hot rock to make a reservoir where none exists, so it can be sited more widely than conventional geothermal and runs through the night. A unit that meets a contract date and bills a customer gives lenders an operating record in place of a pilot. One third of the first phase is running.

HYP: Two further units in commercial operation by 1 January 2027, each built in less than 23 months, would show the method repeating.

1–2 OCT 2026 · EUROPE / AFRICA / LATIN AMERICA · CYBERSECURITY

A ransomware group that favours SharePoint flaws hits a water utility and a telecom operator

OBS: Symantec’s Threat Hunter Team reported on 1 October that a group it calls Longlegs, which it describes as a “China-nexus threat actor,” had attacked at least four organisations in the past two months with Warlock ransomware. They were a water utility, a telecommunications provider, a regional government body and a university, in Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries across Europe, Africa and Latin America. The group “continues to favor SharePoint-related vulnerabilities” for entry. No source names a victim or says whether water or telecom service was interrupted.

Symantec leaves open whether the language pattern reflects “an opportunistic targeting pattern driven by exposed, vulnerable SharePoint servers, or a more deliberate tasking.” The attribution is the vendor’s.

2 OCT 2026 · UNITED STATES · LABOUR MARKET

US payrolls rise by 29,000 in September, below the lowest forecast, and July is revised to a loss

OBS: The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 2 October that nonfarm payroll employment rose by 29,000 in September and that the unemployment rate was 4.2 percent. The revisions carry as much information as the headline: a month first reported as a gain is now a loss.

5 OCT 2026 · STOCKHOLM · NEUROSCIENCE

The medicine Nobel goes to optogenetics, which began with how an alga finds light

OBS: The Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet on 5 October awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine jointly to Karl Deisseroth of the Howard Hughes Medical Institute and Stanford University, Peter Hegemann of Humboldt University of Berlin and Georg Nagel of the University of Würzburg. The citation reads “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics.” Hegemann and Nagel discovered channelrhodopsin, a protein in a single-celled alga that opens a channel when blue light strikes it. Deisseroth put its gene into rat nerve cells and triggered nerve signals with light, publishing in 2005, and two years later made the switch work in the brains of living mice, the press release says.

INF: The prize rewards a research tool. Hegemann and Nagel made the cited discoveries at two Max Planck institutes while studying how algae respond to light, and the release reports that researchers are now using the method “in attempts to restore sight in people with visual impairment.” More than two decades separate the discovery from the prize.

HYP: Regulatory approval of an optogenetic therapy for vision loss would mark the tool’s passage into treatment.

NobelPrize.org · press release, 5 October · NobelPrize.org · prize summary

2 OCT 2026 · NIGERIA · CHILD HEALTH

A nutrient supplement handed out at vaccination visits doubles the odds of measles vaccination in Yobe State

OBS: A cluster-randomised trial published in Nature Medicine on 2 October assigned 20 clusters in Nguru and Karasuwa to routine immunisation or to a strategy that gave a monthly ration of a lipid-based nutrient supplement to children aged 6 to 23 months after routine vaccination. Cécile Cazes of the University of Bordeaux led the study, with co-authors at Yobe State University Teaching Hospital and the medical charity ALIMA. In an endline survey of 1,604 children, the odds of a card-verified first measles dose were about twice as high in the intervention arm (odds ratio 2.08), and coverage rose 20.1 percentage points more than in the control arm.

INF: The trial tests a delivery design: a good that families want is attached to a service they use too little. Only 48 percent of surveyed children in the intervention clusters had ever received the supplement, and the abstract does not separate the effect on recipients from any effect on other children. The outcome is a vaccination card entry, not measles cases.

HYP: Replication by a state health service without the charity’s staff, and a published cost per additional child vaccinated, would show whether the design can run inside a routine programme.

Nature Medicine · Cazes et al., 2 October

2 OCT 2026 · GLOBAL · ECOLOGY

Birds migrate about two weeks earlier than in 1970, which helps them in spring and harms them in autumn

OBS: A study in Nature Ecology & Evolution on 2 October by Jin Liu and Shaopeng Wang of Peking University and colleagues compiled 4,351 population-level estimates of migration timing across 549 bird species and paired them with population trends and rates of climate change at breeding and non-breeding grounds. Migration advanced by a cumulative 13.86 days from 1970 to 2019. Rapid advance “is beneficial for bird abundance during pre-breeding migration but detrimental during post-breeding migration,” the authors write, and the net benefit “depends on pre-breeding advancement outpacing that during post-breeding migration.” The benefit is larger in large-bodied species.

INF: Earlier migration has been read as successful adjustment to warming. The synthesis finds that the same behaviour carries opposite signs in the two halves of the year, so a species can shift quickly and still decline. Monitoring that records only spring arrival misses the autumn cost.

HYP: Monitoring schemes that add autumn departure dates and report both would test the result species by species.

Nature Ecology & Evolution · Liu et al., 2 October

4–5 OCT 2026 · BANGLADESH · PUBLIC HEALTH

Bangladesh’s measles outbreak reaches 1,128 deaths while 5,849 dengue patients are in hospital

OBS: Bangladesh’s combined count of confirmed and suspected measles deaths reached 1,128 on 5 October, of which 102 are laboratory-confirmed, according to health directorate figures reported by The Business Standard. Dengue deaths this year reached 262 on 4 October, with 5,849 patients in hospital, The Daily Star reported; September, with 148 deaths, was the year’s worst month for dengue.

INF: Deaths rose by 84 and suspected cases by 22,524 in 19 days, so the count is rising by more than a thousand suspected cases a day in the seventh month of reporting. There is about one laboratory-confirmed case for every nine suspected ones, so the split between confirmed and suspected deaths reflects testing capacity as well as the disease. The reports give no vaccination coverage for the affected children.

HYP: Daily suspected cases falling below 500, and published vaccination coverage for the affected districts, would show the outbreak turning. A daily count that stays above 1,000 into November would show it has not.

2 OCT 2026 · GLOBAL · HEALTH DATA

Infectious diseases cause fewer than half of deaths in low-income countries for the first time, WHO estimates

OBS: The World Health Organization’s latest Global Health Estimates, released on 2 October, put life expectancy at 73.3 years in 2023, against 73.4 in 2019, UN News reported. Healthy life expectancy was 62.8 years, still 0.4 years below its 2019 level. In 2023, “for the first time,” communicable diseases “accounted for less than half of all deaths in low-income countries.” Noncommunicable diseases caused 74 percent of deaths worldwide, up from 58 percent in 2000.

INF: Health systems in the poorest countries were built and donor-financed around infectious disease. More than half of their deaths now have other causes, a group that includes the chronic conditions that need long-term care. Length of life has recovered from the pandemic faster than healthy life has. The data year is 2023, and the account here rests on UN News.

HYP: Donor and national budgets for low-income countries that add chronic-disease care without cutting immunisation would show systems adjusting. Aid that stays organised around single infectious diseases would show financing lagging the burden.

UN News · Global Health Estimates, 2 October

DEEP DIVE · READ: O

An outbreak and a delivery design

Bangladesh’s health directorate counted 1,128 measles deaths by 5 October, 102 of them laboratory-confirmed, and 197,722 suspected cases since mid-March. A trial published in Nature Medicine on 2 October found that giving families a nutrient supplement after routine vaccination in Yobe State, Nigeria, doubled the odds that a child had a recorded first measles dose.

The trial addresses attendance. In Nguru and Karasuwa, card-verified measles vaccination among children aged 12 to 23 months stood at 29.9 percent in 2022, and 14.2 percent of children aged 6 to 59 months had acute malnutrition. Researchers assigned 20 clusters to routine services or to a strategy that added a monthly ration of supplement. Coverage rose 20.1 percentage points more in the intervention clusters.

Bangladesh’s numbers describe an outbreak already under way. Hospitals have admitted 175,336 patients with suspected measles since March, and 5,849 dengue patients were in hospital on 4 October. Reporting is in its seventh month. The daily bulletins count cases and deaths, and the reports that carry them do not say which children were vaccinated or where coverage fell.

The two reports concern different conditions for controlling measles. A vaccine works only if a child reaches the clinic, and the Yobe trial gave families a second reason to come. An outbreak can be steered only if the health service knows who was missed, and Bangladesh’s published figures separate confirmed from suspected cases without showing vaccination status.

By January, a state health service running the Yobe design without the charity’s staff would show it can work inside a routine programme. Vaccination coverage published by district in Bangladesh would show whether the outbreak follows gaps in coverage. A daily count still above 1,000 suspected cases in November would show a response that is not yet reducing transmission.

1–5 OCT 2026 · EAST AND SOUTHERN AFRICA · EL NIÑO

One seasonal outlook warns southern Africa of drought and Tanzania of floods, and Kenya pre-positions food in ten counties

OBS: The South-West Indian Ocean Climate Outlook Forum’s outlook for October 2026 to March 2027, published by the World Meteorological Organization on 1 October, expects drier conditions and higher drought risk in South Africa, southern and central Mozambique, Malawi and south-western Madagascar. The regional outlook turns that into opposite emergencies in one season, so food reserves and relief budgets may be drawn on for drought and flood at once. Kenya, which lies outside the forum’s area, has named counties and pre-positioned stocks.

HYP: Budget lines and trigger thresholds published by Mozambique, Malawi or South Africa before January would show preparation for the drought side. Appeals for relief after crops fail would show the forecast went unused.

3–5 OCT 2026 · NEPAL · FLOOD RECOVERY

Nepal scales back its flood search and seeks Rs559 billion as a study finds most landslides are old ones returning

OBS: Nepal’s disaster council, chaired by Prime Minister Balendra Shah, on 5 October suspended search and rescue after the 26 August Bhotekoshi–Trishuli floods, except in emergencies, and recommended a budget of Rs559.17 billion for 504 reconstruction programmes, the Kathmandu Post reported. The disaster authority had counted 1,453 bodies recovered and about 5,285 people missing as of 26 September. A study in Communications Earth & Environment on 3 October, led by Erin Harvey of Durham University, analysed a 22-year record of landslides in Nepal. Recurrent or persistent landslides account for about 80 percent of all those visible at any time, and only half of new landslide areas lay more than 30 metres from an existing one.

INF: The disaster authority’s chief, Dharmaraj Upreti, said in a 29 September interview that “the state must take the bold decision to relocate settlements where risks have already been identified.” The landslide record supports the premise for slopes: most of the hazard sits at sites already known. The floods and the landslides are different hazards, and the study does not address the August event.

HYP: The share of the 504 programmes that pays for relocation, against rebuilding in place, will show which model the money follows.

2 OCT 2026 · GLOBAL OCEANS / INDONESIA

A satellite-based estimate puts oil slicks at nearly 1.6 million square kilometres a year, with Indonesian waters the largest hotspot

OBS: Oil slicks from ships and offshore platforms cover nearly 1.6 million square kilometres of ocean surface each year, according to a report by the US nonprofit SkyTruth covered by Mongabay on 2 October. SkyTruth used satellite imagery, machine learning and vessel tracking for 2023 to 2025 and attributed 81 percent of the slick area to vessels, mostly cargo ships and tankers, and 18 percent to oil infrastructure. Indonesian waters had the most verified slicks, 823, followed by Malaysia with 214. Most of the discharges are illegal under the MARPOL convention, the report says, and SkyTruth could find ownership records for only 9 of the 20 vessels it flagged as repeat polluters.

INF: Routine dumping of oily bilge water has been banned for decades and went unenforced because it happened out of sight. Ownership was traceable for fewer than half of the repeat offenders, so detection has arrived ahead of the means to hold anyone to account.

HYP: Prosecutions or port detentions by Indonesia or Malaysia that cite satellite evidence would show detection turning into enforcement.

Mongabay · oil slicks, 2 October

30 SEP–2 OCT 2026 · CALIFORNIA

Newsom’s veto message objects to how the nurse-override bill would be enforced, and its labour backers say they will bring it back

OBS: Governor Gavin Newsom’s veto message for AB 2575, dated 30 September, gives two reasons. By barring retaliation “solely” for overriding or relying on a clinical decision support system, the bill “ties the Labor Commissioner’s hands in establishing a violation — a higher bar than other retaliation protections, which simply prohibit action because of a protected activity.” And linking protection to a scope-of-practice determination “puts the Labor Commissioner in the position of adjudicating the standard of care for patients — something the Labor Commissioner does not have the medical expertise or skill to do.” National Nurses United said the veto “gives hospital corporations a green light to discipline nurses who override unsafe artificial intelligence.” In the same release Lorena Gonzalez, president of the California Federation of Labor Unions, said: “We are committed to bringing this bill back next year.”

INF: Friday’s issue read the veto as leaving the reviewer who disagrees unprotected. The message objects to the enforcement design and leaves the principle standing. California has an agency that polices retaliation and boards that police clinical standards, and the bill asked the first to judge what the second knows. Protection for an override needs an adjudicator competent in the judgment being overridden.

HYP: A 2027 bill that uses a “because of” standard and sends the clinical question to a licensing board would test whether the objection was to drafting. A second veto of such a bill would show an objection to the protection.

2–5 OCT 2026 · SOUTH AFRICA

South Africa’s top court is asked whether an unclicked “submit as final” button can keep 181 candidates off the ballot

OBS: The African National Congress has asked the Constitutional Court to overturn an Electoral Court ruling that it did not submit candidate lists in six municipalities, because it failed to click “submit as final” on the electoral commission’s online nomination system before 5pm on 28 August, the Sunday Times reported on 4 October.
